Cambridge A2 · The derivation

From force balance to Hall voltage

|VH| = B In t e
  1. eE = evB ⇒ E = vB

    At equilibrium, the transverse electric and magnetic forces are equal and opposite.

  2. |VH| = Ew = vBw

    The Hall voltage is across width w, perpendicular to the current.

  3. I = nAve = n(wt)ve

    The cross-sectional area for the current is A = wt.

  4. v = I/(nwte) ⇒ |VH| = BI/(nte)

    Substitute for v. The width w cancels.

Do not confuse w and t. The Hall voltage is measured across w. Thickness t is parallel to B. Here e = |q|; in the syllabus magnitude equation, VH = BI/(ntq), q is the charge magnitude.

04 · Predict, then test

Direction

Reverse the current. What changes?

Keep B and everything else fixed. Predict the signs on both edges before trying it.

Reveal explanation

The magnetic force reverses, so the charge separation and Hall voltage polarity reverse. The voltage magnitude stays the same. Reversing both I and B restores the original polarity.

Proportionality

Make the sample twice as thick.

Keep I, B and n fixed. Does the Hall voltage double, halve or stay unchanged?

Reveal explanation

It halves. A larger cross-sectional area means a smaller drift speed for the same current. Since E = vB, the transverse electric field and Hall voltage are smaller.

Understanding

Do the electrons stop at equilibrium?

Watch stage 3. Identify which component of the resultant force becomes zero.

Reveal explanation

No. The transverse forces balance, so there is no further net sideways drift. Carriers still drift along the sample and the current continues. The existing edge charge maintains the Hall field.

Teacher notes · conventions, assumptions and syllabus
  • For the default case: I to the right and B out of the screen. Electrons drift left. Their magnetic force is down, so the bottom edge becomes negative and the top positive. The electric field points down; the electric force on electrons points up.
  • With fixed current and B, changing carrier sign reverses carrier drift and Hall voltage polarity, but not the magnetic-force direction. The force is q(v × B).
  • The meter displays V(top) − V(bottom). With signed current rightwards, B outwards and signed carrier charge q, this is −IB/(nqt). The textbook magnitude is BI/(nt|q|).
  • Animations show average drift, not microscopic thermal motion or individual collisions. Particle size, excess edge charge, deflection and build-up time are exaggerated. Force arrows are schematic; numbers use SI calculations. The sample drawing does not rescale with thickness or density.
  • Only the excess charge at the edges is drawn; a neutralising background is implicit. A single dominant carrier type is assumed. The illustrative density is suitable for a low-carrier-density sample, not a typical metal.
  • The longitudinal electric field driving the current is not drawn. Voltage leads are treated as ideal and do not draw current.
